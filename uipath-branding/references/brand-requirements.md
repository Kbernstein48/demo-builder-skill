# UiPath Brand Requirements

Source: UiPath Brand Book V3.3, 2026. This file is a condensed operational summary for agents; it is not a replacement for official Brand Center assets or Creative Studio review.

## Brand Foundation

- Values: Humble, Immersed, Bold, Fast.
- Voice: human, direct, non-jargony, warm, friendly, empathetic, down-to-earth, and engaging.
- Writing rules: get to the point, lead with the why and the audience benefit, keep content skimmable, use short sentences and paragraphs, use white space, and say something interesting.
- Avoid tech-speak, lectures, ego, empty jargon, and copy that starts with product features before audience needs.

## Logo

- The UiPath corporate logo is the highest-level brand mark.
- Bundled logo assets are indexed in [logo-assets.md](logo-assets.md). Read that file before selecting production logo files.
- Use the white-out logo on colored backgrounds.
- Use the black logo on white or light backgrounds when contrast requires it.
- The all-orange logo is reserved for digital use cases only; do not use it in print.
- Maintain clear space around the logo on all sides. Minimum clear space is defined by the size of the "U" in the logo.
- Minimum recommended logo size: 80 px for digital formats or 1 in / 25 mm for print.
- Logo may sit in any of the four corners, or centered at the top or bottom, depending on layout and text alignment.
- When possible, ad text should align no higher than the bottom of the logo.
- On photography, use the white-out logo in a darker, less busy corner.
- Do not place the logo too close to other graphics. Increase clear space in dense layouts.
- Do not crop, stretch, recolor, retype, or alter the logo.
- Use logo lockups sparingly. Use only files provided by UiPath Creative Studio. Do not build custom lockups.
- Co-branding pairs the UiPath corporate logo with partner logos using balanced optical alignment, equal-dimension areas when heights match, and a dividing line for stacked layouts.
- Use the glyph only in space-constrained or UI-focused environments such as app icons, favicons, and internal tools where brand recognition is already established.
- For third-party profile images, use the squared-corner glyph to avoid platform-applied rounded-corner issues.
- Glyph sizes: 128 px, 64 px, and 32 px.

### Color Rules

- Color hierarchy is about purpose, not prominence.
- Bright White is a canvas, not an emphasis color.
- Use Robotic Orange for impact, Deep Blue and Agentic Teal for contrast and structure.
- Black is used as backdrop for platform-related content.
- Deep Blue is reserved for brand-related content.
- Secondary and tertiary colors support hierarchy, section differentiation, charts, diagrams, and highlights.
- Pair saturated colors with Deep Blue, Black, or Bright White for legibility.
- Avoid overusing Robotic Orange so it remains attention-grabbing.
- Cool tints work with Agentic Teal; warm tints work with Robotic Orange.
- Cool and warm colors can combine when gamma differences create contrast without clashing.

## Typography

### Primary Typefaces

- Poppins: Black, Bold, Semi Bold. Free via Google Fonts.
- Urbane Rounded: Medium, Demi Bold. Commercial Adobe Fonts typeface, generally for designers and approved brand materials.
- Inter: Regular, Bold, Extra Bold. Free via Google Fonts.

### Campaign Typography

- Poppins Black leads large-scale campaign headlines, often all caps.
- Urbane Rounded Medium supports subheadlines and callouts.
- Inter Regular supports body copy.
- Poppins campaign tracking: -45. Kerning: Metrics. Leading generally follows about 1.2:1; adjust to avoid crowding.
- The Poppins full stop should be square where applicable in Adobe tools.

### General Typography

- Headlines: Poppins Bold, sentence case, tight leading, tracking -45 when supported.
- Subheadlines: Urbane Rounded Medium, sentence case, auto leading/tracking.
- Body: Inter Regular, auto leading/tracking.
- Always check that ascenders and descenders do not collide.

### Web Typography

- Headline: Poppins Bold, tight leading, tracking -45 when supported.
- Subheadline: Urbane Rounded Medium. Alternative: Poppins Bold or Semi Bold.
- Body: Inter Regular.
- The web team decides whether punctuation is needed. If used, use the square full stop when the system supports it.

### Canva Typography

- Use preset Canva template fonts from the brand team.
- Canva ad headline default: Inter Extra Bold, tight leading, tracking -45.
- Canva subheadline/body: Inter Regular.
- Do not replace preset template fonts unless directed by the brand team.
- Do not use punctuation at the end of the sentence in Canva ads.

### PowerPoint and Backup Typography

- Arial is the approved font for PowerPoint decks and is also the backup when brand fonts are unavailable.
- Use Noto Sans TC for Traditional Chinese and Noto Sans SC for Simplified Chinese.
- PPT big numbers/data points: Arial Black, character spacing condensed by about 20 points.
- PPT headlines: Arial Bold, tight character spacing, line spacing multiple 0.8.
- PPT body: Arial Regular, normal character spacing, line spacing 1.0.

### CTAs

- CTA buttons use Poppins Semi Bold, sentence case, tracking -25.
- Use Inter Bold when the brand typeface is not available, especially for social, Canva, and web.
- Keep CTA text two or three words long.
- Approved CTA color combinations: white on Robotic Orange, Robotic Orange on white, black on Agentic Teal, or white outlined on dark backgrounds.
- Maintain equal padding around CTA text.

## Graphics

### Pixel System

- Pixels are modular units inspired by automation: simple pieces coming together to create something greater.
- Use pixels to create structure, rhythm, contrast, flow, and visual metaphors for orchestration.
- Treat pixels as intentional building blocks, not decorative shapes.
- Maintain consistent pixel sizing. Randomly mixing large and small pixels disrupts rhythm.
- Avoid Tetris-like grids and patterns that compete with content.
- Pixels should lead the eye and support the message.

### Pixel Colors

- Use solid fills from the core palette.
- Primary pixel colors: #2D373C, #D9D9D9, #A5E2F0, #15839A, #CE340B.
- Secondary pixel colors: #3CB5C2, #FB6B45.
- Never use white pixels.
- Never place the UiPath logo on #A5E2F0.
- Avoid off-brand color combinations.

### Outlined Pixels

- Outlined and gradient pixels are expressive treatments for high-impact layouts.
- Match outline and text color for clarity: white with white, black with black.
- Gradient strokes can pair with either white or black depending on contrast.
- Reserve advanced outlined/gradient pixel treatments for UiPath Creative Studio or trusted external design partners.

### Otto Brand Avatar

- Otto represents UiPath's agentic foundation and should be used only for community programs, developer-facing events, and internal communications.
- Use Otto selectively and intentionally. Never use Otto as filler or background decoration.
- Match Otto's expression to content tone.
- Do not randomly rotate, crop, distort, or otherwise alter Otto.

### Icons

- Bundled white and dark-grey 96 px SVG icon assets are indexed in [icon-assets.md](icon-assets.md), with a full searchable catalog in [icon-index.md](icon-index.md) and [icon-index.json](icon-index.json).
- Icons convey simple meaning and complement the graphic language.
- Use Agentic Teal with agent-face icons for agents.
- Use Robotic Orange with robot-face icons for robots.
- Use Bright White or light neutrals with human icons for people.
- Source icons from the UiPath icon library or UiPath PowerPoint template when available.

## Photography

- Photography should feel authentic, intentional, emotionally grounded, human, focused, and real.
- Use real-world subjects, environments, and situations that support the message without feeling staged or generic.
- Choose images with clear composition, natural contrast, and visual space for headlines, pixels, and graphic elements.
- Urban landscape: use cities, architecture, infrastructure, and built environments with clear perspective and enough visual space.
- Nature: use open or atmospheric landscape-led visuals with clear composition, natural contrast, a focal point, or leading lines.
- People: show natural collaboration, focus, curiosity, and problem-solving.
- Avoid cluttered cropping, unnecessary photo details, washed-out images, off-palette accent colors, staged poses, forced expressions, and overly polished stock imagery.
- Use standard-license Adobe Stock images, not editorial-use-only images, when selecting stock.

## Brand Use

- Signature experiences and events can use high-energy color, massive type, large blocks, gradients, Otto, and immersive scale.
- Campaigns align messaging, visuals, and content across channels for a specific business outcome. Do not reuse one-off campaign visuals outside their original context unless approved.
- Social media should be scroll-stopping, colorful, and paired with pause-worthy copy.
- White papers use vibrant colors, graphic elements, signature charts, tables, and graph treatments to make complex stories clear.
- Documents use defined hierarchy, standardized typography, approved color application, clear image direction, grids, X spacing, alignment, and whitespace.
- Reports prioritize comprehension. Use color to highlight key insights, not decorate.
- Zoom backgrounds should look polished and subtly branded.
- Awards, badges, office materials, video, and audio should use approved files and packages.
