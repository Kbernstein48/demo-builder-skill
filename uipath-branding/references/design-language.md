# UiPath Design Language

Use this file to translate the UiPath brand requirements into practical design decisions.

## Brand Feel

UiPath design should feel bold, clear, structured, human, and useful. The visual system balances a high-energy tech brand with down-to-earth communication:

- Bold: oversized type, confident color, strong hierarchy.
- Human: warm copy, real people, natural photography, usable layouts.
- Structured: grid-based composition, pixel modules, disciplined alignment.
- Fast: short copy, skimmable sections, direct CTAs, clear next actions.
- Immersed: customer benefit first, technology second.

## Layout Direction

- Use a strong grid and visible hierarchy before adding brand expression.
- Build compositions from blocks, whitespace, and modular pixel motifs.
- Let content breathe. Bright White is an active part of the brand system.
- Use large headlines and compact supporting copy.
- Keep the message dominant when using pixels, photography, or gradients.
- Use asymmetry and corner placement for momentum, but keep alignment deliberate.
- In dense product UI, reduce expressive brand elements and use the palette for hierarchy, status, and emphasis.

## Color Recipes

### Core UiPath

- Background: Bright White or Deep Blue.
- Headline: Deep Blue or Bright White.
- Emphasis: Robotic Orange.
- Secondary emphasis: Agentic Teal.
- Supporting neutrals: #F6F6F6, #D9D9D9, #9D9D9D.

### Agentic Automation Story

- Robots: Robotic Orange.
- Agents: Agentic Teal.
- People: Bright White on dark surfaces, or Black/Deep Blue on light surfaces.
- Use all three intentionally in headline phrases or diagram labels when explaining "agents, robots, people."

### Campaign or Social

- Use stronger contrast and brighter fields.
- Pair Robotic Orange with Deep Blue, Black, Bright White, or Agentic Teal.
- Use tertiary tints to support depth, but keep primary colors dominant.
- Reserve gradients for high-impact, brand-led moments.

### Reports and Data

- Use neutrals for structure and secondary information.
- Use orange, teal, or purple only to identify meaningful categories or key findings.
- Testing Purple is reserved for testing-related content.
- Do not use color only for decoration.

## Typography System

- Use Poppins Bold for most headlines when available.
- Use Poppins Black for high-impact campaign headlines.
- Use Urbane Rounded sparingly for subheads, callouts, and softer contrast when the font is available and appropriate.
- Use Inter Regular for body copy in digital contexts.
- Use Arial in PowerPoint, shared documents, and environments without brand fonts.
- Keep headlines short and benefit-led.
- Use tight headline spacing and leading when supported by the target medium. If a platform or higher-priority instruction disallows negative tracking, keep letter spacing at 0 and preserve impact through weight, scale, and line-height.
- Avoid long all-caps text outside campaign moments.

## UI and Web Adaptation

- Start with usability, then add brand expression.
- Use Inter for dense app text unless Poppins is already part of the app system.
- Use Robotic Orange for primary actions, important highlights, or focal numbers. Do not flood the interface with orange.
- Use Agentic Teal for agentic AI, assistants, agents, intelligence, and automation orchestration concepts.
- Use Deep Blue and neutrals for navigation, containers, dividers, and technical structure.
- Use pixel motifs as small structure cues, page headers, or empty-state accents. Avoid placing decorative pixels inside every card.
- Use the bundled icon index for available white and dark-grey 96 px SVG icons. Select white icons for dark/saturated backgrounds and dark-grey icons for light backgrounds.
- Use accessible contrast for every text, icon, and CTA pairing.
- For buttons, prefer short labels with familiar icons when appropriate.
- Do not recreate official logos in code. Use supplied official assets only.

## Slides and Documents

- Use Arial unless an official template supplies brand fonts.
- Use large, direct headlines and clear section hierarchy.
- Use X/grid spacing consistently.
- Use orange for the key point, not every point.
- Use teal when the content is about agents or AI-agent behavior.
- Use people-focused imagery for customer stories, case studies, and everyday business documents.
- Use report colors to clarify findings; avoid decorative chart palettes.

## Campaign, Social, and Event Creative

- Push scale, contrast, and energy more than in product UI.
- Use bold blocks, oversized type, pixel compositions, and high-impact color fields.
- Use short copy that makes a point quickly.
- Create a clear "made you look" moment without sacrificing readability.
- Avoid reusing campaign-specific visuals outside their original initiative unless approved.
- Use Otto only for approved community, developer-facing event, or internal contexts.

## Photography Direction

- Choose photos that feel candid, purposeful, and real.
- Prefer strong perspective, natural contrast, clear subject hierarchy, and open areas for type or pixels.
- Use urban environments for infrastructure, scale, and business context.
- Use nature for open, atmospheric, or expansive messages.
- Use people for collaboration, focus, curiosity, problem-solving, and customer impact.
- Reject photos that are cluttered, washed out, overly staged, overly polished, or dominated by off-palette accent colors.

## Logo and Asset Discipline

- Use the bundled logo inventory at `references/logo-assets.md` to select the right file for digital RGB, print CMYK, white-out, black, or orange usage.
- Use official UiPath logo, glyph, icon, Otto, award, badge, video, audio, and template assets only.
- Do not draw, trace, recolor, crop, distort, or rebuild official marks.
- Use the white-out logo on colored or photo backgrounds; use the black logo on white/light backgrounds.
- Keep clear space based on the logo "U" and increase it in dense layouts.
- Use the glyph only for space-constrained or UI-recognition contexts.
- Do not create custom lockups. Route lockup needs to UiPath Creative Studio.

## Review Checklist

Before finalizing, verify:

- The work has a clear grid, hierarchy, and whitespace.
- The color choices express meaning: orange for robots/impact, teal for agents, white for canvas, deep blue/black for structure.
- Orange is prominent but not overused.
- Typography is bold and readable, with short headlines and skimmable support copy.
- Pixels are intentional, consistently sized, and not Tetris-like.
- Photography is authentic, composed, high-contrast, and useful for the message.
- CTAs are two or three words and use an approved color pairing.
- Logos and official assets are approved files and are not modified.
- The tone is human, direct, warm, non-jargony, and benefit-led.
- The design can scale across formats without losing recognizability.
